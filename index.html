The Artist

Ronnie Wells works in the language of movement.

He has spent a lifetime watching things that don’t hold still — birds coming off a slough, a horse gathering itself, the way a person’s hands keep talking after the words stop — and then asking bronze to remember it.

It’s patient work. A piece begins in clay, gets argued with for weeks, and only then heads to the foundry to be cast the old way, lost-wax, one pour at a time. What comes back is meant to feel less like a statue and more like a moment somebody finally managed to hold onto.

That instinct runs from tabletop wildlife editions all the way up to the monuments — pieces like the Heart of a Champion tribute and the NatureWorks waterfowl, where the goal is the same at any scale: catch the life, not just the likeness.

What the Studio Makes

From a mantel piece to a town square.

01

Wildlife Bronze

Waterfowl, game, and the quiet animals in between — studied from life and cast so the motion reads from across the room. Available as one-offs and limited editions.

02

Monuments & Portraits

Life-size and larger figures for parks, campuses, and memorials. We work from photographs, stories, and the people who knew them — like the Heart of a Champion tribute.

03

Private Commissions

Have a subject in mind? Bring it. We’ll talk it through, sketch it in clay, and build the piece with you from first idea to final patina. No two leave the studio the same.
